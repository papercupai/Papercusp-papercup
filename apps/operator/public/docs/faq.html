Common questions

Start here for a short explanation, then follow the linked guide for steps. For your account’s current price, permissions or workspace status, check the app or ask Papercup to look it up with your authorization. Available options depend on your installation and account.

What is Papercusp?

Section titled “What is Papercusp?”

Also asked as: What does this app do? How do I use Papercusp?

Papercusp helps you plan work, run agents in a connected workspace and inspect their results. Start with one small task you can check, such as improving a README. A completion label alone does not prove that a result works or has been published. Try your first task.

What is Papercup?

Section titled “What is Papercup?”

Also asked as: Who is the assistant? Can I ask the chat for help?

Papercup is the assistant you can ask for explanations and help with work. Tell it which workspace and project you mean and what result you want. Its ability to act depends on the access and tools available to that session. Learn how to ask for a task.

What is a workspace?

Section titled “What is a workspace?”

Also asked as: Where does my work run? Is a workspace the same as a project?

A workspace is the connected environment where agents do their work. A project is the body of work you select within that environment. Check both names before approving changes; selecting a different workspace does not automatically move your project there. Understand workspaces and agents.

Do I need the desktop app?

Section titled “Do I need the desktop app?”

Also asked as: Can I use a browser? Should I install Papercusp on my computer?

The portal lets you access the options offered by your account in a browser. Local work needs a connected computer with Papercusp installed. Use the installation or download flow offered by your account; portal and desktop controls may differ. Choose your way to use the app.

Does closing the browser stop my agents?

Section titled “Does closing the browser stop my agents?”

Also asked as: Can I close this tab? Will remote work stop when I disconnect?

Closing a browser tab does not itself stop work running on another machine. Ask explicitly to pause or stop and check the acknowledgment and current state. Local work also depends on its computer staying on and connected. Follow and stop work.

Which cloud option should I choose?

Section titled “Which cloud option should I choose?”

Also asked as: What is managed hosting? What does bring your own cloud mean?

Use Papercusp’s cloud means Papercusp manages the host. Bring your own cloud uses your Google Cloud project or AWS account and requires provider setup. Compare responsibility and charges before choosing; an option being described here does not mean it is enabled for every account. Compare cloud workspaces.

Is my workspace ready when its card appears?

Section titled “Is my workspace ready when its card appears?”

Also asked as: Why can I see a workspace but not use it? Is provisioning finished?

A card appearing is not proof that its host is connected and ready. Read the current provisioning and connection status before starting work. If an attempt reports an error, inspect it rather than creating duplicate hosts. Recover from connection problems.

Do I create an account every time?

Section titled “Do I create an account every time?”

Also asked as: My identity already exists; what now? How do I sign in again?

Create your account once, then use Sign in with the same identity provider. If the identity is already registered, repeatedly choosing Create account will not repair access. Connecting Gmail or Calendar is a separate operation. Sign in and check account access.

Why can I not see my organization’s workspace?

Section titled “Why can I not see my organization’s workspace?”

Also asked as: Where is my team’s project? Does signing in give me access to everything?

Check that you are using the intended account and organization. Signing in does not grant access to another organization’s machines or every project. If the expected access is missing, ask the organization administrator to check your role; do not borrow someone else’s credentials. Understand account and organization access.

What can I be charged for?

Section titled “What can I be charged for?”

Also asked as: Does a subscription include cloud costs? Who pays for model use?

Papercusp subscriptions, model use and cloud-provider resources are separate things to check. Read the current price, any model-use allowance shown, and billing responsibility for your account and chosen hosting route. An absent allowance does not mean unlimited use. Your own cloud resources are billed through your provider account; this guide promises no fixed price or unlimited allowance. Check accounts and billing and cloud costs.

Does returning from checkout mean payment is confirmed?

Section titled “Does returning from checkout mean payment is confirmed?”

Also asked as: I paid but access is still restricted; why? Is the checkout success page enough?

Wait for the portal’s subscription confirmation and read its current status. Returning from checkout alone does not prove that payment and entitlements have been confirmed. Preserve the non-secret reference and time if something looks wrong; avoid starting duplicate subscriptions. Resolve payment or access problems.

Does stopping a workspace make it free?

Section titled “Does stopping a workspace make it free?”

Also asked as: Will stop remove all charges? Do stopped disks still cost money?

Stopping and deleting are different actions. A stopped host may still leave billable disks, snapshots or networking resources. Check the provider’s current resource list and charges; do not assume that a stopped state means zero cost. Understand stopping and cloud costs.

What should I check before deleting a workspace?

Section titled “What should I check before deleting a workspace?”

Also asked as: Can I recover after deletion? Is deleting the same as stopping?

Identify exactly what will be deleted, what may remain and who can perform the action. Establish a completed backup and its applicable restore procedure before relying on recovery. Do not assume that deleting a host cancels a subscription or removes every provider resource. Review cloud lifecycle actions and prepare a backup.

Who can access my data?

Section titled “Who can access my data?”

Also asked as: Can other people read my project? Where is my work stored?

Start by identifying the workspace and computer running the task, then check its organization membership, connected apps and access scopes. Access and storage depend on that installation and its configured services; this answer cannot establish your current permissions or a universal retention policy. Ask for an authorized current explanation before sharing sensitive material. Review remote access.

Where should I enter credentials?

Section titled “Where should I enter credentials?”

Also asked as: Can I paste an API key into chat? Should I send my token to Support?

Use the installation’s supported credential or connection setup. Keep secrets in secure configuration, outside public repositories and reports. Do not paste passwords, tokens, session cookies or verification links into chat or support messages. A limited app key should not be replaced with an owner key to bypass a refusal. Set up scoped access.

What does AUTO let an agent do?

Section titled “What does AUTO let an agent do?”

Also asked as: Can an agent act without asking me? Does AUTO remove permission checks?

AUTO lets an agent continue within your approved scope without asking at each ordinary step. It does not remove permissions, security boundaries or the requirement to verify its result. State the task and publication limits clearly, and ask explicitly to pause or stop when you need to reconsider. Understand agent modes.

How do I know a task is finished?

Section titled “How do I know a task is finished?”

Also asked as: What does done mean? Is a passing test proof that my change is live?

Open the actual result and compare it with the agreed acceptance conditions. Read what changed, what was checked and what remains unverified. Implemented, verified and shipped are different claims; publication needs verification at the destination you requested. Review plans and work.

What if an agent makes a mistake or gets stuck?

Section titled “What if an agent makes a mistake or gets stuck?”

Also asked as: How do I interrupt an agent? Why has progress stopped?

Ask it to pause or stop and confirm that it has done so. Request its work-item reference, next concrete action and blocker, then answer any outstanding approval in the same session. Inspect the affected result before choosing recovery; do not make a destructive restore merely to clear an error. Troubleshoot stalled work.

Is my conversation a backup?

Section titled “Is my conversation a backup?”

Also asked as: Can I restore from the chat history? Does a snapshot protect everything?

A conversation is not a project or workspace backup. Source-control history, workspace backups and cloud snapshots protect different things. Check what was included, that creation completed and how the chosen backup will be restored; a restore may overwrite newer work. Test a supported separate restore destination when possible. Plan backups and recovery.

How do I get help safely?

Section titled “How do I get help safely?”

Also asked as: What should a support report include? What do I do if setup fails?

Use the help destination offered by your installation. Include what you tried, the error, its time and a non-secret reference. Remove credentials, verification links and private project or tenant content. For cloud setup, inspect the failed attempt before creating more resources. Find the relevant recovery steps.

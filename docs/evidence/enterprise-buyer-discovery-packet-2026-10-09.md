## Initial segment and workflow hypothesis

Proposed segment: developer-tools or infrastructure teams that repeatedly turn customer-support or operational issues into engineering work. Proposed buyer: an engineering/support leader, with IT/security and finance as approvers. This hypothesis reuses Papercusp's existing Slack/source-ACL/corpus, plan/recipe, audit and budget foundations rather than demanding a new enterprise connector before discovery.

Proposed workflow: intake one permission-authorized issue; gather allowed discussion and repository context; produce a cited problem summary, reproduction/next-action checklist and proposed owner; route the output to the department's reviewer. Initial authority is preparation/review. A customer reply, assignment, code change or production action requires the separately approved workflow authority. The real buyer determines whether this reduces useful work or merely adds another review queue.

## Interview and evidence intake

Capture one record per authorized conversation. Leave unknown fields explicitly unknown:

| Field | Required record |
| --- | --- |
| Provenance | Organization, respondent role and buying/approval authority, conversation date, permission to use the notes, attributable source/ref, interviewer, and what was actually said versus inferred. |
| Workflow baseline | Last three representative cases; frequency; current systems; accepted output; person who decides acceptance; handling/review/rework time; avoid collecting confidential samples before data scope is approved. |
| Identity and actions | IdP/group administration, service identities, delegated administrators, sensitive actions, distinct required approvals, revocation expectation. |
| Data and deployment | Approved sources/ACLs, classification, model/provider restrictions, inference/embedding/log/backup routes, region, hosting profile, private connectivity, retention/delete/restore and keys. |
| Integration demand | The one connector needed first; permission change/freshness requirements; write permissions separately from reads; rate-limit and recovery expectations. |
| Procurement and operations | Required security questionnaire, qualified assurance, legal/DPA/subprocessors, installation/MDM, support coverage, recovery/availability commitments and each accountable approver. |
| Economics | Actual funded budget and purchasing process; model/hosting/subscription classes; maximum exposure; willingness to pay for the accepted outcome; evidence distinct from hypothetical pricing. |
| Decision | Continue, narrow, choose another workflow, or stop; named decision maker; evidence behind decision; next dated action. |

Questions: Which repeated case costs the most review effort? What would count as an accepted result? Which action must never happen without another person's approval? What data/model/hosting choice is mandatory? What would procurement reject today? Who owns the budget, and what would make them pay or decline?

## Proposed pilot scorecard for P-003 review

These are proposed thresholds, not a commitment or measured result. The owner and actual buyer must record the final scope before implementation:

- Evaluate at least 30 comparable cases with a recorded baseline and reviewer acceptance decision; retain case-level evidence and explain excluded cases.
- Proposed utility threshold: at least 80% accepted outputs, with median reviewer effort at least 30% below the matched baseline. Record rework and recurring use separately.
- Any unauthorized action, source-ACL breach or restricted-content canary disclosure stops the trial pending owned remediation and verification.
- Costs must remain inside the explicitly funded allowance; retain model, hosting, subscription and human-review costs separately. Missing provider bills or human measurements stay unknown.
- Stop or narrow when a required connector, deployment, legal, security or support prerequisite cannot be met inside the selected pilot boundary.
